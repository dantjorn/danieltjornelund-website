Drawing No. 030 — Method

How I think about transformation

I work at the intersection of strategy and execution — at inflection points where growth stalls, complexity increases, or the organization's capability no longer matches its ambition.

Five recurring challenges

These are not sequential steps — they are the challenges that most often stand between a scale-up and its next stage of growth. Which ones apply, and in what combination, depends on the company. Some show up alone. Most show up two or three at a time.

Founder-led → Professionally managed

Not about hiring executives. About building a system: clear ownership, strong middle management, and operating cadence with accountability. Without this, growth breaks under its own weight.

Strengthening the value chain

Execution, not structure alone, determines outcomes. Growth fails when the chain is misaligned. Making the system work end-to-end — product through delivery through commercial execution — so each function reinforces the next.

SAM to TAM

Expansion is not about volume. It is about earning the right to move — product, then customer, then geography — without breaking focus or economics.

Professionalizing the board–CEO relationship

As companies scale, governance becomes a constraint. Clarity on expectations, decision rights, and performance tracking. A strong board–CEO relationship is not political — it's operational.

Vanity metrics to financial performance

Early growth hides behind activity. Scale requires financial clarity — customer behavior, unit economics, and the path from execution to EBITDA.

Drawing No. 031 — Diagnosis

Not every company is fighting the same battle

Before prescribing a fix, the first task is diagnosing which of these five situations an organization is actually in. The stages describe increasing complexity and risk — not a sequence every company must pass through.

+L1 · OPTIMIZATION

Execute better

PMF proven. The work is discipline, not redesign.

PMF proven, infrastructure in place. The work is GTM efficiency and operational discipline — not redesign.

+L2 · SCALING

Institutionalize the basics

The model works. The infrastructure hasn't caught up.

The model works, but the management layer and infrastructure haven't caught up to the business's needs yet.

+L3 · EVOLUTION

Extend the model

Healthy core, but every market has a ceiling.

The core is healthy and funds the future — but every addressable market has a ceiling. Optional, deliberate expansion.

+L4 · TRANSFORMATION

Redesign while running

Not optional. The future must be built in time.

The future must be built before the existing business declines too far. Not optional — existential.

+L5 · REINVENTION

Save it, then rebuild it

Rescue first. Growth comes second.

The legacy solution can no longer support the business. The task is rescue first — stabilize, then re-grow.

+THE MISTAKE

Mismatched hire

Fit matters more than pedigree.

An optimizer hired for L4 tightens efficiency while the model decays. A transformer hired for L1 redesigns what didn't need it. Fit matters more than pedigree.
